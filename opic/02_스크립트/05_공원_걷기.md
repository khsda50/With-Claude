# 공원 가기 + 걷기 (통합 스크립트)

서베이의 **여가: 공원 가기** 와 **운동: 걷기** 를 이 한 파일로 커버한다.
두 항목이 같은 활동이므로, 준비량은 절반이지만 출제 확률은 두 배다.

---

## 내가 준 재료

| 항목 | 내용 |
|---|---|
| 누구와 | **집 근처 사는 친구** |
| 언제 | **주말 밤**, 약 1시간 |
| 어디 | **발바닥 공원** (집 근처) |
| 공원 구성 | 운동기구, 연못, 산책로 |
| 사람들 | 걷거나 운동기구에서 운동 |
| 걸으며 하는 것 | **친구와 대화**. 끝나고도 그 대화를 생각한다 |
| 사건 | **다른 동네 친구들을 자주 마주친다** ⭐ |

**핵심 영어 표현**
- 발바닥 공원 → `Balbadak Park` + `"balbadak" means the sole of your foot`
- 산책로 → `a walking path` / `the loop`
- 야외 운동기구 → `outdoor exercise equipment`
- 연못 → `a pond`

---

## Q1. 언제, 누구와, 얼마나 걷나요 (습관)

> *"When and how often do you go walking? Who do you go with?"*

```
I walk about once a week, usually on a weekend night, and I always go with the
same friend — he lives right near me.

We usually go pretty late, after dinner, so around nine or ten. And we walk for
about an hour. It's not really exercise in the serious sense. We're not trying
to burn calories or hit a step count or anything. We just walk and talk.

Actually, that's the real reason we do it. If we just wanted to catch up, we
could sit somewhere. But it's different when you're walking. There's no table
between you, nobody's looking directly at each other, and somehow that makes it
easier to talk about things. Some of the conversations I remember most happened
on that walk.

The weekend part isn't really a choice, though. During the week I don't get home
until around eleven at night, so there's no version of my schedule where a
weekday walk is possible. So it's Saturday or Sunday night, or it doesn't
happen.
```

**점수를 버는 지점**
- `It's not really exercise in the serious sense.` → **정의를 부정하며 시작.** 그다음 설명이 자연스럽게 이어진다
- `There's no table between you, nobody's looking directly at each other, and somehow that makes it easier to talk` → **관찰.** 이 문장 하나가 답변의 급을 올린다
- `there's no version of my schedule where a weekday walk is possible` → 직장 스크립트의 야근과 연결
- `So it's Saturday or Sunday night, or it doesn't happen.` → 짧은 마무리

## Q2. 그 공원을 묘사해 주세요 (묘사)

> *"Tell me about the park you go to. What does it look like?"*

```
The park I go to is called Balbadak Park, and it's about a ten-minute walk from
my apartment.

The name is kind of funny, actually. "Balbadak" means the sole of your foot in
Korean. So it's basically "Sole of the Foot Park." I've never actually asked why
it's called that. I assume it's because it's a walking park, but honestly,
nobody around here questions it anymore.

It's not a huge park, but it has everything you'd need. There's a walking path
that loops all the way around, there's a pond in the middle, and there's a
section with outdoor exercise equipment — the kind you find in every Korean
neighborhood park, where you push and pull metal bars.

At night, it's mostly people doing exactly what I'm doing. Walking, usually in
pairs, going around the loop. And there are always a few people on the exercise
machines, mostly older people. It's quiet, but it's never empty.

That's what I like about it, actually. It's not a place you go to see anything.
It's just where the neighborhood goes to move around a little.
```

**점수를 버는 지점**
- `"Balbadak" means the sole of your foot in Korean.` → **모르는 사람에게 설명.** 채점자가 좋아하는 순간이다
- `I've never actually asked why. I assume it's because…` → 추측 표현
- `the kind you find in every Korean neighborhood park, where you push and pull metal bars` → 관계사로 정의
- `It's quiet, but it's never empty.` → 대구
- `It's not a place you go to see anything. It's just where the neighborhood goes to move around a little.` → **마무리 통찰**

## Q3. 왜 걷나요 / 걸으면서 뭘 하나요 (이유 · 의견)

> *"Why do you like walking? What do you think about while you walk?"*

```
Honestly, the walking itself isn't really the point for me. The conversation is.

The whole hour is basically one long conversation with my friend. We talk about
work, about people we both know, about whatever's going on that week. And
afterwards, on the way home, I usually keep thinking about what we talked about.
Sometimes for the rest of the night.

That's the part I didn't expect, actually. I thought walking would clear my
head. It doesn't, really — it fills it. But in a good way. It's the one time in
the week when I'm actually thinking about something other than work.

The other reason I've stuck with it is that it's easy. I don't have to book
anything, I don't have to change clothes, I don't need any equipment. I put on
shoes and I'm out the door. I've tried other kinds of exercise before and I
always quit after a couple of months. This one I've kept doing, and I think
that's exactly why.
```

**점수를 버는 지점**
- `the walking itself isn't really the point. The conversation is.` → **첫 문장에서 예상을 뒤집는다**
- `I thought walking would clear my head. It doesn't, really — it fills it.` → **최고의 문장.** 대비 + 자기 수정 + 통찰이 한 줄에 다 있다
- `I don't have to…, I don't have to…, I don't need…` → 부정 병렬로 리듬
- `I've tried other kinds of exercise and I always quit… This one I've kept doing, and I think that's exactly why.` → 현재완료 + 인과 마무리

## Q4. 산책 중 기억에 남는 일 (과거 경험) ⭐

> *"Tell me about a memorable experience you had at the park."*
> *"Did anything unexpected ever happen while you were walking?"*

**집 스크립트의 "25년 같은 동네"와 여기서 연결된다.**

```
The thing that happens most often on that walk is that I run into people I know.
And I mean people I haven't seen in years.

I've lived in this neighborhood since I was in elementary school — about
twenty-five years — so a lot of the people I grew up with are still around here.
Or their parents still live here, so they come back on weekends.

There was one night in particular that I remember. My friend and I were doing
our usual loop, and someone walking the other way stopped and said my name. And
it took me a second, because it was dark and I genuinely didn't recognize him at
first. Then I realized it was a friend from middle school. I hadn't seen him in
probably ten years.

So the three of us just stood there on the path for twenty or thirty minutes,
talking. In the middle of a park, at night. And then he went his way, we went
ours, and honestly, I haven't seen him again since.

That's the strange thing about walking around the neighborhood you grew up in.
You're not really going anywhere, but you keep running into your own past.
```

**왜 이 답변이 강한가**

| 지점 | 효과 |
|---|---|
| `And I mean people I haven't seen in years.` | 자기 수정으로 강조 + 현재완료 |
| `I've lived in this neighborhood since I was in elementary school` | **집 스크립트와 고리를 맞춘다.** 이야기가 하나로 이어진다 |
| `it took me a second, because it was dark and I genuinely didn't recognize him at first` | 구체적 순간 |
| `I hadn't seen him in probably ten years.` | **과거완료** |
| `In the middle of a park, at night.` | 짧게 끊어 장면 강조 |
| `You're not really going anywhere, but you keep running into your own past.` | 마무리 |

> **마지막 문장이 부담스러우면** 이렇게 바꿔도 된다:
> `That's what I like about living in the same place for so long. You never know who you'll run into.`

## Q5. 예전과 지금 비교 (고난도) — **빈칸 채우기**

> *"How has the park changed compared to the past?"*
> *"How has your exercise routine changed over the years?"*

25년을 같은 동네에서 살았으니 이 문항은 오히려 유리하다. **아래 중 사실인 것을 골라 채운다.**

```
① 공원이 예전엔 어땠는지 기억나는 게 있다면
   → "Back then, it was just an empty lot / it didn't have the exercise equipment /
      the pond wasn't there. These days…"

② 예전엔 다른 운동을 했다면
   → "When I was in school, exercise meant sports — soccer, basketball, that kind of
      thing. It was always a group activity. These days it's just walking, and it's
      much more about clearing my head than being fit."

③ 예전엔 더 자주 걸었다면 / 지금 일 때문에 줄었다면
   → "I used to go a lot more often. But since work got busier, it's become a
      once-a-week thing. That's probably the biggest change."
```

## 발음 주의

- `Balbadak Park` — 한국어로 발음하면 된다. 지명은 원래 그렇다
- `pond` [판드] — `pound` 와 헷갈리지 않게
- `equipment` [이**퀴**프먼트] — 복수형이 없다. `equipments` ❌
- `neighborhood` [**네**이버훗]
