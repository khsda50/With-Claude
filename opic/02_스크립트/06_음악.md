# 음악 감상하기

취미 항목 중 가성비 최상. 그리고 **이번 시험에서 감정적으로 가장 강한 카드**가 여기 있다.

---

## 내가 준 재료

| 항목 | 내용 |
|---|---|
| 요즘 듣는 것 | **마이클 잭슨** |
| 계기 | **영화 〈마이클〉을 보고 빠졌다** |
| 언제 | **통근 시간 (편도 2시간, 하루 4시간)** — 사실상 여기서만 듣는다 |

## Q1. 어떤 음악을 좋아하나요 (의견)

> *"What kind of music do you like? Who is your favorite artist?"*

```
These days I've been listening to Michael Jackson, almost exclusively.

And there's a specific reason for that. A while ago I watched a movie about
him — it was just called "Michael" — and after that I completely fell into it. I
went home and started going through his albums in order, and I've basically been
on that ever since.

What surprised me was that I already knew almost every song. I didn't have to
learn any of them. They were just... already in there. There's a reason for
that, actually, but it's a longer story.

As for why I like it — his music is old now. Some of it is forty years old. But
it doesn't sound old at all. The rhythm is so strong that it's physically hard
to sit still. And even if your English isn't great, you can tell exactly what he
means, because it's in how he sings it, not just the words.

So that's been in my ears for months now. Almost nothing else.
```

**점수를 버는 지점**
- `almost exclusively` → 강한 부사 하나로 문장이 올라간다
- `They were just... already in there.` → **말을 멈췄다가 이어가는 연출.** 유창성 신호
- `There's a reason for that, actually, but it's a longer story.` → **Q3에 대한 예고.** 채점자가 다음 답변을 기대하게 만든다
- `it doesn't sound old at all` → 예상 반박
- `even if your English isn't great, you can tell exactly what he means` → **자기 상황과 연결.** 시험장에서 특히 잘 먹힌다

## Q2. 언제, 어떻게 듣나요 (습관)

> *"When do you listen to music? How do you listen to it?"*

**이 답변의 무기는 통근 2시간이다.**

```
Honestly, almost all of my music listening happens in one single place — on the
train.

My commute is about two hours each way, so that's four hours a day. And that is
basically my music time. I put in my earbuds — I use Galaxy Buds — open my
streaming app, and that's the ride. Sometimes I'll watch something on Netflix
instead, but most of the time it's music.

Everywhere else, I actually don't listen much at all. At work I can't, because I
need to concentrate, and if there are lyrics I end up listening to the lyrics
instead of doing my job. When I go walking on the weekend, I'm with my friend,
so we're talking the whole time. And at home I'm usually doing something else.

So it's kind of a strange situation, now that I say it out loud. Music is a big
part of my life, but it basically only exists inside those four hours on public
transportation.

I use an app called FLO — it's a Korean streaming service. I don't own any music
anymore. I just pay every month and everything's there.
```

**점수를 버는 지점**
- `in one single place — on the train` → 첫 문장에서 답을 고정
- `two hours each way, so that's four hours a day` → **계산해서 보여준다**
- `At work I can't, because… When I go walking… And at home…` → **세 경우를 부정으로 나열.** 대비가 선명해진다
- `now that I say it out loud` → **말하면서 깨닫는 연출.** 준비된 답이 아니라는 인상을 준다
- `Music is a big part of my life, but it basically only exists inside those four hours` → 마무리 통찰

## Q3. 음악과 관련해 기억에 남는 것 (과거 경험) ⭐⭐ **최고의 카드**

> *"Tell me about a memorable experience related to music."*
> *"Is there a song that reminds you of something?"*

**콘서트를 안 가봤어도 전혀 문제없다. 이 답변이 콘서트 이야기보다 훨씬 강하다.**

```
There's one thing about listening to Michael Jackson that I really didn't
expect.

When I started going through his songs after that movie, I kept having this
feeling that I already knew them. And not just the famous ones — all of them.
It took me a while to figure out why.

It was my father. When I was a kid, he used to hum Michael Jackson songs around
the house. All the time. While he was doing something in the kitchen, or getting
ready in the morning. He never sat me down and played me an album or anything
like that. He just hummed. And I heard it for years without paying any attention
to it at all.

So now, all these years later, I'm sitting on a train at eleven at night
listening to these songs — and what I'm actually hearing is my father, in our
old apartment.

I hadn't thought about that in a really long time. And I don't think he even
knows he did it. I've never asked him about it.

It's a strange thing, honestly. I found this music through a movie. But it turns
out I've known it my whole life.
```

**왜 이 답변이 이번 시험 최고의 카드인가**

| 지점 | 효과 |
|---|---|
| `I kept having this feeling that I already knew them.` | **미스터리로 시작.** 채점자가 답을 기다린다 |
| `It was my father.` | **세 단어 문장.** 긴 문장들 사이에서 강하게 꽂힌다 |
| `he used to hum… around the house. All the time.` | **used to = 과거 습관.** IH 판정의 핵심 문법 |
| `He never sat me down and played me an album. He just hummed.` | 부정 → 긍정 대비 |
| `what I'm actually hearing is my father, in our old apartment` | **현재와 과거가 한 문장에 겹친다.** 이런 문장은 IM에서 절대 안 나온다 |
| `I hadn't thought about that in a really long time.` | **과거완료** |
| `I found this music through a movie. But it turns out I've known it my whole life.` | **현재완료 마무리.** 이야기가 한 바퀴 돌아 닫힌다 |

> **이 답변 하나에 시제가 다섯 개 들어 있다.**
> 현재 / 과거 / 과거 습관(used to) / 과거완료 / 현재완료.
> 채점자가 IH를 주기 위해 확인해야 하는 것들이 전부 여기 있다. **이 답변만큼은 완벽하게 만든다.**

## Q4. 예전과 지금의 음악 감상 비교 (고난도 · 비교)

> *"How has the way you listen to music changed?"*

```
The way I listen to music has completely changed, and honestly it happened
pretty fast.

Back then — when I was in school — you had to download songs. You'd get an MP3
file, put it on a device, and that was your music. And because of that, you were
limited. You only had whatever you'd downloaded, maybe a few hundred songs, and
you listened to those over and over. If you wanted something new, you had to go
and get it.

These days I don't own a single song. I pay a monthly fee, and basically
everything ever recorded is just there. I can think of a song and be listening
to it three seconds later.

Obviously that's better in most ways. But I do think we lost something. Back
then, because your music was limited, you actually knew it. You knew every track
on the album, in order. Now I have access to everything, and I mostly listen to
the same twenty songs, and I skip constantly. I don't think I've listened to a
full album properly in years — except for Michael Jackson recently, which might
honestly be the first time in a long time.

So, more access, less attention. That's the trade, I think.
```

**점수를 버는 지점**
- `Back then… / These days…` → 시제 대비
- `You'd get an MP3 file, put it on a device, and that was your music.` → `would` 로 과거 습관
- `I can think of a song and be listening to it three seconds later.` → 구체적 과장
- `Obviously that's better in most ways. But I do think we lost something.` → **양면 평가.** IH 이상의 신호
- `except for Michael Jackson recently` → Q1·Q3과 고리를 맞춘다
- `more access, less attention. That's the trade.` → **압축 마무리**

## 콘서트 질문이 나오면 (안 가봤을 때 대처)

> *"Tell me about a concert you've been to."*

**절대 "I've never been" 하고 끝내지 않는다.** 이렇게 돌린다.

```
Honestly, I've never been to a big concert. It's one of those things I keep
saying I'll do and never actually do.

The main reason is timing. The artists I like either don't tour here, or — in
Michael Jackson's case — obviously that's not possible anymore. He died when I
was in school. So the one person I'd most want to see live is the one person I
definitely can't.

That's actually kind of a sad thought, now that I say it.

But it's on my list. There's a musical I've been wanting to see, and I keep
telling my girlfriend we should go. So maybe this year.
```

**이렇게 하면 "경험 없음"이 오히려 좋은 답변이 된다.**
- 이유를 설명하고 → 감정을 붙이고 → **미래 계획으로 닫는다**
- `the one person I'd most want to see live is the one person I definitely can't` → 가정법 + 대구

## 발음 주의

- `exclusively` [익스**클**루시블리] — 어려우면 `almost only` 로 바꿔도 된다
- `rhythm` [**리**듬] — h는 소리 없다
- `hum` [험] — 흥얼거리다. **이 스크립트의 핵심 동사.** `used to hum` 을 20회 반복해 둘 것
- `FLO` / `Galaxy Buds` — 그대로 말하면 된다
