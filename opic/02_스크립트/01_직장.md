# 직장 콤보 (사업/회사)

서베이에서 `사업/회사` 를 골랐으므로 **묘사 → 습관 → 경험** 3문항이 세트로 나온다.

> ## ⚠️ 최대 원칙 — 전문 용어 금지
>
> 채점자는 반도체를 모른다. 아래 단어를 말하는 순간, 그 문장은 채점에서 사라진다.
>
> | 금지 | 대체 |
> |---|---|
> | verification / UVM / testbench | `I test the design on a computer` |
> | RTL / circuit design | `the design`, `the circuit` |
> | simulation | `a test`, `running a test` |
> | coverage / waveform | `the data`, `the results` |
> | burst / single / transaction | `big chunks` / `tiny pieces` |
> | SoC / IP / subsystem | `a chip`, `a part of the chip` |
>
> **어려운 걸 쉽게 설명하는 능력이 그대로 언어 점수가 된다.** 이건 손해가 아니라 이득이다.

---

## Q1. 회사와 하는 일 (묘사)

> *"You indicated that you work. Tell me about your company and what you do there."*

```
Sure. I work at a company that designs semiconductor chips — the kind of chips
that go inside things like cars and phones.

My job is to check those designs. The idea is actually pretty simple. Making a
chip is extremely expensive, and once it's manufactured, you can't fix it. If
there's a mistake, you throw everything away and start over. So before that
happens, my job is to test the design on a computer and find the problems early.

What I actually do all day is try to break the design on purpose. I put it in
strange situations and check whether it still behaves the way it's supposed to.

My team has about twenty people. It's a pretty talkative team, which surprises
people — I think most people expect engineers to just sit quietly at their
desks. But we can't really work alone. My part connects to someone else's part,
so we're constantly checking things with each other.

As for the office, it's nothing special — just desks in a row. The only thing
I'd mention is that I use three monitors. That sounds like a lot, but when
you're comparing a design against the test results, you need the space.
```

**점수를 버는 지점**
- `The idea is actually pretty simple.` → 어려운 주제를 여는 만능 문장
- `you throw everything away and start over` → **이유를 끝까지 설명함.** 사실 나열과의 차이
- `which surprises people — I think most people expect engineers to just sit quietly` → 예상되는 반응을 먼저 언급. IH 전개
- 사무실 묘사에서 **모니터 3대**처럼 구체적 숫자 하나가 답변을 살린다

## Q2. 하루 일과 (습관)

> *"Tell me about a typical day at your work, from morning to evening."*

```
Okay. So first of all, my company is in Pangyo, which is south of Seoul, and I
live in the northern part of the city. So my commute is about two hours. Each
way. Yeah — four hours a day, on public transportation.

We have flexible hours, so technically I can come in whenever I want. But I end
up getting there around nine almost every day anyway. Old habits, I guess.

The first thing I do is step outside for a few minutes to get some air, and then
I come back and go through my email. That's kind of my warm-up before I start
anything real.

Mornings are mostly meetings. Like I said, our work is really connected, so
there's always something to discuss with the design team. Then I have lunch at
the company cafeteria, which is honestly one of the better things about the job.

The afternoon is when I actually get things done. That's when I sit down by
myself, put my headphones on, and work. I barely talk to anyone for a few hours.

Leaving time really depends on the project. Lately it's been around nine at
night, which... is not great. By the time I get home it's almost eleven. So
that's probably the hardest part of my job right now — honestly, not the work
itself, but the hours.
```

**점수를 버는 지점**
- `about two hours. Each way. Yeah — four hours a day.` → **짧게 끊어 강조.** 원어민이 실제로 쓰는 리듬
- `Old habits, I guess.` → 짧은 자기 논평. 유창성 인상을 크게 올린다
- `not the work itself, but the hours` → **대비 구조로 마무리.** 이 한 줄이 문단을 닫는다

> **담배 부분:** 스크립트에는 `step outside for a few minutes to get some air` 로 써뒀다.
> 사실대로 `step outside for a smoke` 라고 해도 감점 요인이 전혀 아니다. 편한 쪽으로 쓰면 된다.

## Q3. 기억에 남는 프로젝트 · 어려웠던 일 (과거 경험) ⭐ **핵심 문항**

> *"Tell me about a difficult or memorable experience you had at work."*

**이 답변이 이 시험에서 당신의 최고 카드다. 반드시 전부 과거형으로.**

```
Yeah, there's one that I still think about. It was about three years ago, on a
project for a car chip.

So, we were running tests, and everything was passing. On paper, there was no
problem at all. But I noticed that one of the tests was taking much longer than
it should have. It still passed, so technically nobody had to care about it. But
it bothered me, so I decided to look into it.

Honestly, seeing what was wrong was the easy part. Understanding why it was
happening took me almost half a day of digging through the data. What I found
was that the information was supposed to move in big chunks, but somewhere along
the way it was getting broken into tiny pieces. That was why it was so slow.

And that's when it got serious. Normally, you would just go back and fix the
circuit. But we were so close to the deadline that changing the design wasn't
really an option — it was way too risky at that point. So for a while, nobody
knew what to do.

What we ended up doing was finding a setting that controlled it, so we could fix
the behavior without touching the actual circuit. After that, the time dropped
by more than half. And then we realized the same thing was happening in other
parts of the chip too, so we fixed those as well.

The reason I remember it is that it almost slipped through. Every single test
said "pass." If I hadn't been curious that day, nobody would have caught it.
```

**이 답변이 강한 이유**

| 지점 | 효과 |
|---|---|
| `It was about three years ago, on a project for a car chip.` | 언제·무엇을 첫 문장에서 고정 |
| `On paper, there was no problem at all. But I noticed…` | **긴장 도입.** 사건이 시작된다 |
| `seeing what was wrong was the easy part. Understanding why… took me half a day` | **현상 vs 원인을 구분.** IH급 사고 표현 |
| `big chunks → tiny pieces` | 전문 용어 0개로 기술 문제를 설명 |
| `changing the design wasn't really an option — it was way too risky` | **갈등.** 이야기에 무게가 생긴다 |
| `nobody knew what to do` → `What we ended up doing was…` | 해결 |
| `If I hadn't been curious that day, nobody would have caught it.` | **가정법 과거완료.** IH 판정에서 가장 강한 신호 중 하나 |

> **마지막 문장은 통째로 외운다.**
> `If I hadn't been 〇〇, nobody would have 〇〇ed.`
> 이 틀은 여행·영화·일상 어떤 경험담에도 갈아 끼울 수 있다.

## Q4. 예전과 지금의 업무 비교 (고난도 · 비교)

> *"How is your work now different from when you first started?"*

```
It's changed quite a lot, actually.

When I first started, about five years ago, I basically did what I was told.
Someone would give me a task, I'd finish it, and then I'd wait for the next one.
I didn't really understand how my part fit into the whole chip.

These days it's almost the opposite. Now I'm usually the one deciding what needs
to be tested, and sometimes I'm the one telling the design team that something
looks wrong. That's a completely different kind of pressure. Back then, if
something slipped through, it wasn't really my responsibility. Now it is.

The other thing that changed is how I think about the job. I used to believe my
job was to make the tests pass. Now I think my job is to find the thing that
everyone else missed. It sounds similar, but it's actually the opposite mindset.

That said, one thing hasn't changed at all — the hours. If anything, they've
gotten worse.
```

**점수를 버는 지점**
- `Back then… / These days…` → 시제 대비가 명확
- `I used to believe… Now I think…` → `used to` 활용
- `It sounds similar, but it's actually the opposite mindset.` → **추상적 통찰.** AL 쪽으로 넘어가는 문장
- `That said, one thing hasn't changed` → 반전으로 마무리
