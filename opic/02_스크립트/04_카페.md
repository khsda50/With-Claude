# 카페 · 커피전문점 가기

묘사 문항에 가장 강한 항목. 그리고 **무인 카페**라는, 설명이 필요한 소재를 갖고 있다.

> **무인 카페는 이 시험에서 유리한 카드다.**
> 채점자는 그게 뭔지 모른다. **모르는 사람에게 풀어서 설명하는 능력**이 곧 언어 점수다.
> 직장 스크립트에서 반도체를 설명한 것과 똑같은 근육을 쓴다.

---

## 내가 준 재료

| 항목 | 내용 |
|---|---|
| 어디 | **집 앞 무인 카페** (단골) |
| 공간 | 좁지만 잘 꾸며놔서 사람이 많다 |
| 자리 | **콘센트 있는 자리** 고정 |
| 모드 A | 주말 데이트 — 밥 먹고 나서 이야기하러, 1~2시간 |
| 모드 B | 주말 낮 혼자 공부 — 2~3시간 |
| 카페에서 공부하는 이유 | 집에선 집중이 안 됨. **특히 강아지가 계속 방해** |
| 음료 | **매일 같은 아이스 아메리카노** |
| 사건 | **옆 테이블에 초등학생 단체 → 너무 시끄러워서 집에 감** |

**핵심 영어 표현**
- 무인 카페 → `an unmanned café` / `there's no staff at all` / `you order from a machine`
- 콘센트 → `a power outlet`

---

## Q1. 자주 가는 카페를 묘사해 주세요 (묘사)

> *"Tell me about the café you usually go to. What does it look like?"*

```
There's one café I go to almost every weekend, and it's right in front of my
apartment building — like a two-minute walk.

The interesting thing about it is that there's no staff. It's completely
unmanned. You walk in, you order from a machine, you pay with your card, and the
machine makes your coffee. Nobody says hello, nobody asks what you want. These
have become pretty common in Korea over the last few years.

The place itself is small — maybe seven or eight tables. But whoever set it up
put real effort into it. The lighting is warm, the furniture actually matches,
and there are plants everywhere. So even though it's basically a machine café,
it doesn't feel cheap at all. That's probably why it's always busy.

I have one specific seat I want, which is the one with a power outlet next to
it. And if somebody's already sitting there when I walk in, honestly, it kind of
ruins my plan. I'll still stay, but it's not the same.

And I order the exact same thing every single time — an iced americano. All year,
even in the middle of winter. I don't think I've ever ordered anything else
there.
```

**점수를 버는 지점**
- `Nobody says hello, nobody asks what you want.` → **부정 병렬.** 무인이라는 개념을 장면으로 설명한다
- `whoever set it up put real effort into it` → 관계사 활용
- `even though it's basically a machine café, it doesn't feel cheap` → 양보 구문
- `if somebody's already sitting there, it kind of ruins my plan` → **사소한 개인 습관.** 이런 게 답변을 진짜로 만든다
- `All year, even in the middle of winter.` → 짧게 덧붙여 강조

## Q2. 카페에서 뭘 하나요 (습관)

> *"What do you usually do at the café? Who do you go with?"*

```
I go there for two completely different reasons, and they don't look anything
alike.

The first one is with my girlfriend, on the weekend. Usually we have lunch or
dinner somewhere first, and then we go to a café just to talk. That's the whole
plan — there's no other purpose to it. We'll sit there for an hour or two,
sometimes longer. And honestly, that's often the best part of the day. The meal
is kind of the excuse; the café is the actual thing.

The second one is by myself, and that's for studying. I usually go on weekend
afternoons and stay about two or three hours. I study English, or I read about
things related to my work.

And the reason I go out to study is pretty simple — I can't concentrate at home.
At all. Part of it is that home is where I relax, so my brain just doesn't
switch over. But the bigger problem is my dog. He will not leave me alone. The
second I sit down at my desk, he's right there. So if I actually need to get
something done, I have to leave the house.

Which is a little funny, when you think about it, because the café is literally
right in front of my building. I walk for two minutes, and suddenly I can focus.
```

**점수를 버는 지점**
- `two completely different reasons` → **두 경우로 쪼개기.** 길이가 두 배
- `The meal is kind of the excuse; the café is the actual thing.` → **대구 문장.** 강한 한 줄
- `He will not leave me alone.` → will을 강하게 써서 습성 표현
- `Which is a little funny, when you think about it, because…` → **자기 상황에 대한 논평.** IH급 마무리

## Q3. 카페에서 있었던 일 (과거 경험) ⭐

> *"Tell me about a memorable experience you had at a café."*
> *"Tell me about a time something unexpected happened while you were out."*

**아이러니가 이 답변의 무기다.** 평소에 좋아하던 "직원이 없다"가 그날은 문제가 됐다.

```
Yeah, there's one afternoon I still remember, and it kind of ruined my whole
plan for the day.

It was a Saturday, and I went to my usual café to study. I got my seat — the one
with the outlet — I got my iced americano, and I had about three hours of work
in front of me. Everything was set.

And then, maybe thirty minutes in, a group of elementary school kids came in.
And not two or three — I'd say seven or eight of them, all together.

They were loud. Really loud. And to be fair, they weren't doing anything wrong.
They were just being kids — talking over each other, moving between tables,
laughing at everything.

But here's the thing. Because it's an unmanned café, there's no staff. There's
nobody there to say anything. Normally an employee would go over and ask them to
keep it down a little. But there was literally no one working there. And that's
usually the thing I like most about that place — and that day, it was the exact
problem.

I tried to keep going for about twenty minutes with my headphones on, and it
just wasn't happening. So I packed everything up and went home. I got nothing
done that day.

Looking back, it's kind of funny. But at the time, I was pretty annoyed.
```

**왜 이 답변이 강한가**

| 지점 | 효과 |
|---|---|
| `I got my seat, I got my iced americano, and I had about three hours in front of me. Everything was set.` | **모든 게 완벽했다는 설정.** 무너질 준비 |
| `And not two or three — I'd say seven or eight` | 자기 수정으로 강조 |
| `to be fair, they weren't doing anything wrong` | **상대 입장 인정.** IH급 균형 감각 |
| `that's usually the thing I like most about that place — and that day, it was the exact problem` | **아이러니.** 이 답변의 심장 |
| `I got nothing done that day.` | 짧은 단정문 |
| `Looking back, it's kind of funny. But at the time, I was pretty annoyed.` | **지금 감정 vs 그때 감정.** 시제 대비 마무리 |

## Q4. 요즘 카페와 예전 카페의 차이 (고난도 · 비교)

> *"How have cafés changed compared to the past?"*

**무인 카페가 이 문항의 완벽한 소재다.**

```
Cafés have changed a lot here, and honestly, the one I go to is a perfect
example of it.

Back then, a café was a place with people in it. Someone took your order,
someone made your coffee, and you'd talk to them a little. And you went there to
meet somebody — you'd sit for thirty minutes and then leave.

These days it's completely different. A lot of the cafés near me have no staff
at all now. You order from a machine, you sit down, and you can be there for
three hours without speaking to a single person.

And that's exactly why people use them differently now. People go to cafés to
work, to study — to be alone in public, basically. If you walk into one on a
weekend afternoon, half the people have a laptop open and headphones on.

I think there are two reasons for the change. One is cost, obviously — hiring
people is expensive. And the other is that a lot of us actually want it this
way. We don't want to interact with anyone. We just want a table, an outlet, and
to be left alone.

I'm one of those people, so I can't really complain. But I do notice that a café
used to be a noisy, social place, and now it's mostly the sound of typing.
Something was traded there, I think.
```

**점수를 버는 지점**
- `Back then… / These days…` → 시제 대비
- `to be alone in public` → **개념을 한 구절로 압축.** 이런 표현이 IH를 넘어 AL 쪽 신호가 된다
- `I think there are two reasons` → 이유를 구조화
- `I'm one of those people, so I can't really complain. But…` → 자기 인식 후 반전
- `Something was traded there.` → 짧고 강한 마무리

## 발음 주의

- `unmanned` [언**맨**드] — 자신 없으면 `there's no staff` 로 풀어 말해도 완전히 같은 효과
- `americano` [어메리**카**노]
- `outlet` [**아**웃렛] — 첫 음절 강세
- `elementary` [엘리**멘**터리]
